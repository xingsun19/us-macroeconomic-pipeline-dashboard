# Empirical findings

The validated snapshot spans January 2000–October 2026 (322 monthly rows), using the FRED vintage dated 2026-11-08.

Unemployment peaked at 14.8% in April 2020; its latest available value is 4.1% (October 2026). The peak identifies the largest labor-market disruption in this sample; three series alone cannot identify its causes.

CPI increased 98.6% between January 2000 and September 2026. The price level and the inflation rate answer different questions: lower positive inflation still means rising prices.

Year-over-year inflation calculated from seasonally adjusted CPI peaked at 8.98% in June 2022; the latest available reading is 3.72% (September 2026). It can differ from the headline unadjusted CPI inflation rate.

The federal funds rate is 3.88% in October 2026, versus a sample maximum of 6.54% in July 2000. Its monthly frequency smooths changes within a month.

The unemployment–inflation Pearson correlation is -0.437 across 320 paired months; the correlation of monthly changes is -0.196. This sensitivity to transformation cautions against treating a full-sample correlation as a stable Phillips-curve relationship.

The latest common observation across unemployment, inflation, and the policy rate is September 2026. The ex-post real-rate proxy is 0.03 percentage points. It uses realized past inflation, not expected future inflation or a structural neutral rate.

## Interpretation limits

These are descriptive associations, not causal estimates or forecasts. Monthly observations are autocorrelated; level correlations may reflect trends, policy endogeneity, and structural breaks. Lag searches are exploratory and involve multiple comparisons. No p-values or causal claims are inferred. FRED may revise historical values. Data are a current-vintage snapshot, not a real-time backtest. Missing/unreleased months are not imputed.
