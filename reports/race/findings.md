# Unemployment by Race & Ethnicity

The 2026-11-09 FRED vintage contains 286 monthly rows from January 2003 to October 2026. All four unemployment series are seasonally adjusted percentages.

White unemployment is 3.7% in October 2026; its sample mean is 5.05% across 285 observed months.

Black or African American unemployment is 6.6% in October 2026; its sample mean is 9.73% across 285 observed months.

Asian unemployment is 2.8% in October 2026; its sample mean is 4.57% across 285 observed months.

Hispanic or Latino unemployment is 4.7% in October 2026; its sample mean is 7.09% across 285 observed months.

The Black–White gap averages 4.68 percentage points across 285 paired months. It is 2.90 pp in October 2026, compared with 5.30 pp in January 2003; its sample maximum is 8.50 pp in August 2011.

Within the Financial crisis and aftermath (2008–2010) comparison window, Black or African American has the largest measured baseline-to-peak increase among these four series: 7.8 pp, from 9.0% in 2007-12 to 16.8% in 2010-03. This is an event-window description, not an estimate of the event's causal effect.

Within the Pandemic shock and aftermath (2020–2021) comparison window, Hispanic or Latino has the largest measured baseline-to-peak increase among these four series: 14.5 pp, from 4.4% in 2020-02 to 18.9% in 2020-04. This is an event-window description, not an estimate of the event's causal effect.

The contemporaneous federal-funds/Black–White-gap correlation in levels is -0.562, using 285 paired months. The policy rate responds to economic conditions, so these associations do not identify policy effects.

The contemporaneous federal-funds/Black–White-gap correlation in monthly changes is 0.046, using 283 paired months. The policy rate responds to economic conditions, so these associations do not identify policy effects.

## Interpretation and methods

Hispanic or Latino is an ethnicity and may include people of any race. The White, Black, and Asian series include Hispanic people. Categories overlap, are not exhaustive, and must not be added or treated as population shares. Each rate is unemployed people divided by that group's labor force (age 16+).

These are descriptive associations, not causal effects. Economic conditions, policy responses, occupational composition, sampling variation, and other omitted factors can influence the patterns. Group averages do not describe every individual.

Financial-crisis comparisons use December 2007 as baseline and January 2008–December 2010 for the peak search. Pandemic comparisons use February 2020 as baseline and March 2020–December 2021 for the peak search. These are stated analysis windows, not official recession dates. Annual means use available months; counts disclose partial years. Gaps require both groups in the same month. No values are imputed. Lagged correlations pair the rate at t with unemployment/gap at t+k on the complete monthly grid. They are exploratory, with no causal identification or multiple-testing-adjusted inference. CPS estimates have sampling error; the four series alone do not provide confidence intervals.
